**The Syon 570 Campaign — Rise of Foxi Co**

In the aftermath of repeated skirmishes between SolFed and the Commonwealth, Foxi Co operated in the margins—salvaging, rebuilding, and quietly expanding while larger powers exhausted themselves.

Following a contested engagement, Foxi Co secured ten damaged Forge units from abandoned or destroyed installations. With only minimal engineering teams and the deployment of a single Rook-class logistics vessel, they initiated a recovery and stabilization mission on a neglected industrial world.

Rather than extracting immediate profit, Foxi Co committed to long-term investment. Over the course of a year, they restored planetary stability, delivered essential aid, and began constructing ground-based shipyard infrastructure. Production capacity expanded rapidly: the equivalent of twelve operational Forges, supported by four Hive-class vessels configured for transport and mining.

This infrastructure triggered an economic revival. Resource extraction surged, exports resumed, and the once-abandoned world became a regional supplier. At this stage, Foxi Co deliberately relocated the original ten refurbished Forges off-world. Publicly, these units appeared barely spaceworthy—dismissed as scrap being cleared from orbit. In reality, they were repositioned to distant systems.

Two newly constructed Forges remained planetside, filling critical industrial gaps. The Hives continued reconstruction support, maintaining the illusion of a purely humanitarian-industrial operation.

As the frontline shifted and SolFed dominance increased in the sector, Foxi Co pivoted diplomatically. They showcased the system’s growing shipbuilding capacity and proposed cooperation—offering to manufacture capital ships, including a Bishop-class and several SolFed battleships. This secured political cover and bought time.

Over the next five years, while fulfilling SolFed contracts, Foxi Co quietly expanded elsewhere. Additional Forges were constructed and exported to the remote system designated Syon 570. By the end of this phase, ten specialized Forges operated off-world, while the original system had become a thriving industrial hub.

This success attracted attention.

SolFed intelligence, driven by both strategic interest and resource demand, began asserting control. Tensions escalated into a localized uprising, aimed at seizing centralized manufacturing authority—particularly for new battleship designs.

Before full conflict erupted, Foxi Co executed a strategic withdrawal. Two Hive vessels were refitted for combat readiness and, along with their Rook, exited the system. Control was transferred to an incoming SolFed garrison, along with intact infrastructure and resources—a calculated gesture of goodwill.

SolFed accepted. The system was stabilized under their authority, producing approximately two battleships annually.

Meanwhile, Syon 570 evolved in secrecy.

The relocated Forges established extensive mining operations and replicated themselves, expanding to a network of twenty Forges. Combined with Hive support, the system developed into a hidden industrial complex. By this stage, Foxi Co controlled the equivalent of thirty Forge outputs and a growing fleet of support and combat vessels.

To defend this expansion, Foxi Co commissioned a protection force: one Bishop, two Rooks, sixteen Knights, and sixty-four elite Pawns. Construction was rapid, but the surge in activity did not go unnoticed.

SolFed demanded control of Syon 570.

Foxi Co responded with deception. Half the Forges were covertly relocated to sympathetic moons under the cover of pirate activity. These hidden sites produced small combat craft and recruited pilots from war-torn systems, gradually forming a decentralized resistance network. Their operations targeted SolFed supply lines, disrupting logistics without revealing the true scale of Foxi Co’s capabilities.

For nearly a decade, this shadow conflict persisted.

Eventually, SolFed escalated. Identifying Syon 570 as a critical node, they deployed a battle fleet, devastating surrounding systems in retaliation for ongoing harassment. Civilian populations suffered heavily, polarizing the region and forcing a confrontation.

Foxi Co set the trap.

The SolFed fleet, depleted from extended operations and missile expenditure, was lured into a direct jump toward Syon 570. Expecting submission, they instead encountered a prepared defense.

Upon arrival, a third of the fleet was destroyed instantly—intercepted by relativistic drone swarms positioned within the system’s gravity well. The remaining forces advanced, only to be halted by a Queen-class battleship absorbing sustained fire without collapse.

Simultaneously, multiple Bishop-class vessels executed coordinated strikes, systematically dismantling the attacking formation. Repeated engagements followed, but SolFed losses mounted—three fleets in total.

Facing mounting pressure from Commonwealth forces elsewhere and unable to sustain further losses, SolFed entered negotiations.

A final engagement near the Ganinil refueling station—featuring a newly deployed King-class destroyer—ended inconclusively, but confirmed mutual exhaustion. Neither side could afford continued escalation.

A peace agreement was reached.

SolFed relinquished governance rights over Syon 570 in exchange for a strategic alliance. Foxi Co agreed to supply non-combat vessels to support SolFed operations on other fronts. As part of the treaty, Foxi Co dismantled its forward base at Ganinil and formally reduced its visible fleet strength.

Both sides understood the reality: continued conflict would result in mutual collapse.

From a handful of salvaged Forges and a single Hive-supported operation, Foxi Co had engineered one of the first successful planetary defenses against a full SolFed battle fleet—not through raw force, but through patience, misdirection, and control of industrial growth.

The lesson was clear.

In this era, a Forge was not just a factory—and a Hive was not just a support vessel.

Together, they were the foundation of power.
