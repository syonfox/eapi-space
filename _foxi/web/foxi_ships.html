FOEI Xylophis

Officially designated the “FOEI Interceptor,” the Xylophis is equipped with four warheads and up to ten missiles. In practice, it functions as a cluster munition the size of a small bus. What it lacks in raw firepower, it compensates for through inexpensive manufacturing and mass deployment. When operated by a coordinated pilot group—or autonomously in networked formations—it can be surprisingly effective. It is primarily deployed in AI-driven drone swarms capable of overwhelming conventional defense systems. Originally designed as Foxi’s passenger shuttle, the Xylophis proved essential to early defense efforts. Its adaptability allowed trade fleets to field large numbers quickly, overwhelming even heavily armed battleships through sheer volume. After suffering multiple engagement losses, the Solare Federation adopted the design, noting its resemblance to OPLI Xylophis manufacturing platforms. They scaled production rapidly and began employing the same swarm-based strategy against advancing adversaries and large hostile formations, successfully holding the line across several contested fronts. Foxi Co. now supplies key industrial components to OPLI factories, most notably an improved missile bay capable of fitting eight missiles into a standard rack previously limited to five.

Additional Lore
Over time, the Xylophis earned a reputation not as a single craft, but as a system of warfare. Entire battle doctrines formed around its deployment, where victory was achieved not through precision strikes, but through saturation and inevitability. Pilots assigned to Xylophis swarms often describe a unique combat experience—less like flying a ship, and more like becoming a node in a living network. Losses are expected, even calculated, but the swarm adapts in real time, learning and shifting tactics mid-engagement. Rumors persist of experimental variants capable of limited self-repair and battlefield resource harvesting, suggesting that future Xylophis deployments may operate indefinitely behind enemy lines, turning attrition itself into a weapon.
